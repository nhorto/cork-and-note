Log-a-wine · UX explorations

Interactive mockups for tightening up the wine-logging flow: a quick-by-default form layout, replacements for the drag sliders, and a rebuilt flavor-note picker. Everything is clickable — styled in the new Spritz palette.

Quick by default, detail on demand

Today every section is fully expanded — wine info, five sliders, the flavor picker, photos, notes — which makes a quick "we're at a tasting, just log it" entry feel like homework. Both variants below keep the fast path (info → stars → save) always visible and tuck the deep-dive sections away until asked for. Collapsed sections show a live summary so you never lose sight of what you've already filled in.

Suggested direction: Variant A (inline collapsibles) is the smaller change and keeps everything on one scroll. Variant B (bottom-sheet sections) makes each deep-dive feel focused and keeps the main screen tiny, at the cost of one extra tap per section. Both move Save into a sticky footer bar so it's always reachable.

Other layout notes baked into both variants: the overall rating becomes tappable stars (matching how it's displayed everywhere else in the app) instead of a slider; Save/Cancel live in a sticky bar so a quick log never requires scrolling past empty sections; the collapsed headers show live summaries ("Tannins 4 · Body 3½", "Cherry, Oak +2") once data exists.

Replacing the drag sliders

The current sliders are continuous 0.1-step drags — hard to hit an exact value, easy to bump while scrolling, and the whole track fights the scroll gesture. Every option below is tap-first (no drag needed, so no scroll conflict) and snaps to half-points, which is as much precision as a tasting note really needs. Try each one.

Suggested direction: the segmented bar (or the dots) for detailed ratings — one tap sets the value, half-step precision is visible at a glance, and there's nothing to drag. The descriptor chips are a strong alternative if we'd rather speak wine ("Grippy" instead of "4.0") — they also make the AI sommelier's suggestions read naturally. If we keep a slider anywhere, it should be the snap-slider version: 0.5 detents, bigger thumb, tap-to-set, and drag only starts on the thumb itself.

A — Segmented bar suggested

Ten half-step cells. Tap (or swipe across) to fill. Reads like a level meter.
Pros · one tap, zero drag; scroll never hijacked; value visible without reading a number; compact — five rows fit one screen.
Cons · half-steps only (fine for tasting); cells are smaller tap targets than chips.

B — Tap dots

Five dots, like a star row. Tap a dot for the full value, tap it again for a half.
Pros · biggest tap targets; matches the star mental model; very fast.
Cons · the tap-again-for-half trick needs discovering (a one-line hint fixes it).

C — Descriptor chips

Speak wine, not numbers. Each characteristic gets its own five words; the value is stored underneath.
Pros · zero ambiguity about what "3.5 tannins" means; friendliest for beginners; pairs beautifully with the sommelier chat.
Cons · five levels only; taller rows; wording needs care (and localization later).

D — Snap slider (fixed, not removed)

Keep the slider but fix it: 0.5 detents with tick marks, a 32px thumb, tap-anywhere-to-set. In the app, drag would only begin on the thumb, so page scrolling can't grab it.
Pros · familiar; keeps the "continuum" feel; least visual change.
Cons · still the fiddliest of the four on a phone; still a horizontal gesture inside a vertical scroll.

E — Stepper

Plus/minus in half-steps. The dead-simple fallback.
Pros · impossible to fat-finger; great accessibility.
Cons · up to 10 taps to reach a value; slowest option; feels clinical.

Note — overall rating

Separate question: the overall rating is displayed as stars all over the app, so the input should just be stars too.

Flavor notes: search that just shows pills

Today, typing "st" gives you four collapsed category headers — Fruit, Spice & Wood, Earth & Mineral, Other — and you have to open each one to find "Strawberry". The rebuild: search results are a flat wall of tappable pills (with a small category hint on each), a Popular tab is the default view, the library grows ~40 notes, and one input does both search and add-custom — if nothing matches, the first pill becomes "Add ‘…’".

Suggested direction: the right-hand phone. Search-as-you-type across all categories with flat pill results, Popular first (the ~30 notes people actually use), category tabs after it for browsing, and the merged search/add-custom input. This also removes a whole input row from the form.
