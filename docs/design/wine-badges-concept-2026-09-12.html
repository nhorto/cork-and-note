Concept & implementation brief · Version 3

A wine journey
worth collecting.

Badges, points and levels for Cork & Note: the wineries you visit, the grapes you keep coming back to, the regions you cross off, and the cellar you look after.

Version 3 revises the Version 2 brief after a full read of the app source. It keeps the family-and-tier foundation, pulls the collectible one-off badges and a points ladder into the first release, replaces the server pipeline with a design sized for this app, and records the owner's decisions.

Recommendation: ship six tiered badge families, a collection of about thirty one-off badges, and a single points total with named levels in the first release. Compute progress in the app from data it already loads. Persist only what was earned and when.

Scope: planning artifact, no app changes made. Version 3 was written after a full read of the current main checkout of Cork & Note on September 12, 2026, including migrations, services and screens. Production data was not inspected. The owner settled the open decisions the same day; they are recorded in §08.

01 / Review of Version 2

What stands, and what changes.

Version 2 was a careful brief. Its research is sound, its counting rules are the right instinct, and its read of the codebase found three real data traps. Where it goes wrong is scale: it designs the award pipeline of a product with millions of users, and it defers the parts that make a badge system fun.

02 / The two deferrals

Why v2 pushed one-offs and points out, and why v3 pulls them back in.

One-off badges

v2's reason: "Three families with four milestones already create twelve separate objects to explain and maintain." It treated every badge as a bespoke thing with its own artwork, copy and rule.

Why that does not hold here: if the catalog is data, a badge is a row: key, name, one-line rule, icon, metric, threshold, points. The evaluator is one function over about eight metric types. Adding "Chardonnay Lover" after "Cabernet Lover" costs a line, not a feature. Artwork is a vector icon inside a tier ring in v1, so there is no illustration backlog either.

Why it matters: the tiered families reward volume. The one-offs reward variety and personality, which is the part people screenshot. "Full Spectrum" or "Virginia Regular" says something about you; "Wine Discoverer · Silver" says how much you logged.

Points, levels and one overall rank

v2's reasons: a global rank needs a formula that weighs unrelated activities; points invite people to grind whatever pays best; a "Gold member" label can read as a paid tier or as expertise.

The constraint that answers all three: points are only ever awarded by badges. There is no per-log point, no per-bottle point, nothing to grind that badges do not already reward. The weighting question becomes "how many points is a Gold tier worth", which is a config value, not a philosophy. The level is a name for a points range and is shown as "Level 4", never as "Gold member".

What you get: a single number that always goes up, a level that gives Home and Profile something to celebrate between badges, and a "next level in 40 points" nudge. Cost once badges exist: one reducer and one line of UI.

Points and levels

How points accrue

Tier awards: Bronze 10, Silver 25, Gold 50, Platinum 100. A completed family is worth 185. Per-grape badges pay 10, 25 and 50 for Fan, Lover and Devotee. One-offs pay 15, 30 or 50 as listed. With six families, eighteen grapes and the launch one-offs the ceiling is about 3,100 points, so the ladder below has headroom.

Points never decrease. Live progress bars can move down if records are deleted; earned badges and their points stay.

Where it lives

Profile gets an Achievements card under the visit stats: level, points, the three most recent badges, and "12 of 41 earned". Tapping opens a full screen with three sections: Families (progress bars), Collection (the five shelves, locked badges greyed with their rule), and History. Home's Your Journey card gains one line: the nearest next milestone.

How it appears

One vector icon per family or badge from the icon set already in the app, inside a ring coloured by tier. Text and shape carry the tier, not colour alone. Locked badges show the icon dimmed and the rule in plain words, so the collection doubles as a list of things to try.

How it celebrates

After a save completes, one bottom sheet: "New badge" with the icon, name, rule and points, plus a level-up line if one happened. Several at once are grouped in one sheet. A haptic tap on open. Dismiss or "See collection". A toggle in Profile settings turns the sheets off.

Deletions and edits

Progress is recomputed from live rows every time, so bars move honestly. Earned badges are never revoked. It is the user's private journal; if they delete a mistaken visit that once tipped them to Silver, Silver stays and the bar shows the true count underneath. This is what Untappd does and it avoids the entire revocation and correction-reason machinery in v2.

Existing users

The first evaluation after the feature ships walks all history and awards everything already earned in one pass, with one grouped sheet: "Recognized from your journal: 9 badges, Level 4". No per-badge celebrations for backfill.

07 / Implementation

Small, testable, no new infrastructure.

Architecture in one paragraph

A catalog file describes every badge as data. A pure evaluator turns the user's visits, wines, cellar lots and consumptions into a facts object, then into progress for every badge. After any save, the app runs the evaluator over the freshly cached data, diffs against the earned rows it has, inserts the new ones into a single owner-scoped table, and shows one sheet. The screens only read. Nothing on the server has to know what a badge is.

If badges ever become public or comparable, move the evaluator into an edge function then. The catalog and evaluator are plain JavaScript and would move without change.

Data model

New table user_achievements: user_id, badge_key, tier (null for one-offs), points, earned_at, seen_at, source ('live' or 'backfill'). Unique on user, key and tier. Row-level security: owner reads and inserts, nobody updates points, delete-account already cascades through user-owned tables.

New preference: a boolean for celebration sheets, stored where the other profile toggles live.

That is the entire migration. No pending-evaluation table, no rule versions, no revocation columns.

08 / Decisions

Settled by the owner on September 12, 2026.

  1. Points and levels ship in v1.
  2. Level titles are bottle sizes: Split, Half Bottle, Bottle, Magnum, Jeroboam, Methuselah, Salmanazar, Balthazar, Nebuchadnezzar.
  3. Per-grape badges are tiered at 3, 10 and 25 tastings: Fan, Lover, Devotee.
  4. Launch grape set left to the author: the twelve most-logged international grapes plus six Virginia grapes, listed in §03.
  5. Region Explorer counts US AVAs only for now. Non-US regions (France, Spain, Italy and others) are wanted later, once a region layer exists for them.
  6. A bottle opened at home is not a winery visit. The pre-fix lands first.
  7. Earned badges are never revoked.

Next: this brief is committed under the repo's research docs, an epic with four sub-issues is opened against issue #104, and work starts with the pre-fix.
