# Cork & Note: Facebook and Instagram campaign plan

**September 9, 2026 · Creative planning draft**

Start by testing two reasons to use Cork & Note: **remember the wines you like** and **get approachable personal wine help**. Use winery visits and dinner at home as additional settings to explore once the first messages are understood.

This plan accompanies the [positioning report](positioning-and-messaging-report-2026-09-09.md) and the [interactive ad gallery](../../mockups/ad-campaigns/index.html). The six creative directions are review options; the initial paid pilot should use a small shortlist. The existing Virginia plan supplies a possible starting geography, while the broader brand welcomes casual wine drinkers wherever they try a bottle.

No campaigns have been created or launched. Budget, launch timing, audience preference, and brand voice remain open. The working assumptions are an iPhone-first US launch, a warm and approachable voice, and an adult audience aged 21 and over.

## 1. What the first campaign needs to learn

The first useful questions are:

1. Does remembering a favorite wine create a stronger reason to try the app than personal AI assistance?
2. Can a casual drinker understand the benefit without already knowing wine terminology?
3. Does the person who responds to the ad actually save a meaningful wine note or find the assistant useful?

The advertisement should make one promise and supply one piece of evidence. A small label can identify the app as a journal with an AI sommelier; the entire feature list does not need to appear in each ad.

Use the ordinary moment as the hook. Examples: forgetting the name of a bottle, having a question about a flavor, or wanting to remember a winery favorite. Avoid claiming perfect recommendations, complete knowledge of a person's palate, or guaranteed improvements in wine expertise.

## 2. The six concepts

IDs match the gallery and its feedback exports. Primary text below is the common core; add the appropriate prelaunch or launch sentence in section 3.

| ID | Message and headline | Visual direction | Hypothesis and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A01** | Memory: **Loved the wine. Forgot the name?** | Cream editorial still-life, bottle, handwritten note | A familiar memory problem gives people an immediate reason to keep a journal. Final proof should show a saved wine and the user's reaction. |
| **A02** | Personal help: **Wine questions? Bring them along.** | Bordeaux background and a simple conversation | An ordinary question makes AI assistance understandable. Replace the scripted example with an actual successful app conversation before publishing. |
| **A03** | Discovery: **Good wine. Your words.** | Rosé palette, large type, simple tasting-word labels | Using familiar words makes note-taking feel accessible. The user's own reaction remains central. |
| **A04** | Winery visits: **That winery. That wine. Saved.** | Sage vineyard postcard and a short note | A winery visit gives the journal a concrete occasion. An illustrated vineyard does not imply a named winery partnership. |
| **A05** | Product proof: **Keep more than a bottle photo.** | Parchment background and a real app screenshot | The actual entry screen makes the value of notes and ratings tangible. Prefer a current release capture when producing the final ad. |
| **A06** | At home: **A little help choosing tonight.** | Dark evening table, bottle, simple place setting | Choosing an owned bottle for dinner makes cellar assistance relevant beyond collectors. Confirm Tonight's Pick's final plan access before publishing. |

### A01 — The forgotten favorite

**Primary text:** That bottle you loved over dinner deserves a place in your notes. Save the wine and your own reaction with Cork & Note, your personal wine journal.

**Supporting copy:** Keep the bottle, your rating, and a few words to remember it.

**Link headline:** Remember your next favorite.

### A02 — The approachable sommelier

**Primary text:** “I like this wine, but how would I describe it?” Bring your questions to a friendly AI sommelier, and keep your own tasting notes in the same app.

**Supporting copy:** Your personal AI sommelier. A little help exploring what you like.

**Link headline:** Meet your personal wine companion.

The gallery's answer is illustrative copy. It is not evidence of a production response or a claim that a structured tasting course exists. Test and capture the interaction first.

### A03 — Your taste, your words

**Primary text:** “Fruity.” “A little sharp.” “Would try again.” Start with your own words. Cork & Note keeps your wine notes together, with an AI sommelier for the questions along the way.

**Supporting copy:** Start with what you notice. Keep a note. Ask a question.

**Link headline:** Get to know your taste.

### A04 — The winery memory

**Primary text:** Keep a little more of your winery day. Save the wines you tried, what you thought of them, and the place you want to remember with Cork & Note.

**Supporting copy:** Keep the places you visit and the wines you loved there.

**Link headline:** Bring your tasting notes home.

### A05 — More than a bottle photo

**Primary text:** Your camera roll remembers the label. Add your own rating and notes with Cork & Note, so you can remember what made the wine worth saving.

**Supporting copy:** Save the wine. Remember what you thought.

**Link headline:** A personal journal for your wines.

### A06 — A little help tonight

**Primary text:** Something to go with dinner? Tonight's Pick can suggest a bottle from your Cork & Note cellar, using the food, occasion, or mood you choose.

**Supporting copy:** A suggestion from the bottles you already have at home.

**Link headline:** Meet Tonight's Pick.

General chat and Tonight's Pick receive different context in the current implementation. Use the named feature for claims about selecting owned bottles. Do not extend that claim to every chat.

## 3. Match the offer to the launch stage

| Stage | Offer and action | Destination | What success means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before the app is available | “Coming soon to iPhone. Get launch updates.” A Learn more or suitable signup action, as supported by the chosen campaign setup | A working launch signup page or configured lead form with an actual follow-up process | A qualified, reachable prospective user; track whether they later activate after launch |
| App available | “Download free for iPhone. Optional Pro; limited AI use included free.” An install action where supported | The published iPhone listing, with appropriate app-campaign setup | An install followed by a saved wine, helpful assistant use, or useful return visit |
| People who already showed interest | A concrete demonstration they have not yet seen | A destination matching their stage | Progress toward a first useful action; use only when a sufficient measurable audience exists |

The gallery defaults to **Before launch**. Its signup and install buttons are visual previews. It does not create a signup service or connect an ad account. The existing marketing site had no launch signup when reviewed.

Use a Leads-oriented setup for a genuine prelaunch signup objective; consider App promotion once the listing and app measurement are ready. Confirm the objective, destination, and eligible optimization events in the actual account when configuring the campaign. A store-link click is not an install, and a website signup is not a paid subscriber.

Keep the ad and landing-page promise consistent. An ad offering approachable help should lead to a page that explains the assistant early. A winery ad can emphasize visits while still explaining that the journal works at home.

## 4. Audience and campaign structure

For the first pilot, use **one clearly documented launch stage, one geographic scope, and two or three creative options**. A01 and A02 are the recommended starting pair, provided A02's demonstration is validated; A05 is a useful additional candidate if budget allows.

If the Virginia focus remains, use it consistently when comparing messages. A broad US campaign can be a later comparison. Testing A01 nationally and A04 only in Virginia would mix the effect of the message with the effect of geography.

Plan for US adults aged 21 and over. Treat age and country as actual delivery constraints to verify in the account. Avoid relying on an optional audience suggestion to enforce them. Check current platform rules and final placement eligibility at setup; this is an operational targeting choice, not a completed policy review.

Begin with a reasonably broad eligible audience inside the chosen geography. Add wine interests only if they are available and a clear reason supports using them. No interest targeting or audience size has been verified in an ad account. Do not split a small budget into many combinations of geography, age bands, interests, placement, and creative.

Start by comparing the **whole creative package**: headline, image treatment, and supporting copy. This is exploratory and cannot tell you which individual component caused a difference. Once a direction shows promise, compare two headlines using the same visual, offer, and audience. Keep other major changes out of that comparison.

A campaign that lets the platform allocate delivery among ads will generally produce uneven exposure. Treat its early results as directional. Use the platform's experiment capability for a cleaner comparison when available and when budget and volume justify it; small samples do not support confident causal conclusions.

## 5. Budget options

These are illustrative caps to choose between, not recommended acquisition-cost benchmarks or a spending commitment. They refer to **Meta media spend only**; account separately for creative production and any Apple Search Ads budget from the older plan.

| Available test budget | Example pacing | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not decided | Review concepts and observe a few users before buying traffic | Shortlist two messages, validate claims, prepare destination and measurement |
| About $300 | $20/day for 14 days = $280, leaving $20 unallocated | Two concepts within one geographic scope; focus on qualitative learning and initial funnel data |
| About $500 | $30/day for 14 days = $420, leaving $80 unallocated | Two or three concepts; use the reserve only if there is a clear reason to extend or refine the pilot |
| About $1,000–$1,500 | Initial 14 days at $40/day = $560; reserve the balance for a second, informed round | Test a small shortlist, then refine the strongest direction or examine a new audience separately |

Use a total cap you are comfortable losing as a learning expense. Daily pacing is a planning average; configure the available total-budget controls and monitor actual spend. Do not assume a stated daily amount alone enforces the total cap.

Check delivery, destination functionality, and event reporting early. Pause promptly for broken links, unavailable offers, misleading crop results, or nonfunctional measurement. Avoid declaring a creative winner from a few clicks or changing it every day. At the end of a fixed round, record what was learned even if the sample is inconclusive.

Do not adopt the older report's acquisition-cost and conversion estimates as targets for this app. There is no verified campaign baseline yet.

## 6. Produce images after the creative review

The HTML gallery deliberately uses editable SVG illustrations, original typography, and an embedded existing screenshot. No new AI lifestyle images were generated for this round.

The recommended production sequence is:

1. **Choose the combination.** Record message ID, visual ID, palette, AI emphasis, stage, format, and any custom headline. Save two or three combinations in the gallery and export the feedback.
2. **Generate scene options.** Use the selected image brief to create a small number of lifestyle or still-life backgrounds. Favor a believable home table, small tasting notebook, or relaxed vineyard setting over displays of prestige.
3. **Add exact brand and copy separately.** Use the approved existing mark and typeset the words in the composition. Add real app captures as separate layers. Avoid asking an image model to invent app interfaces or render final text.
4. **Verify product demonstrations.** Replace the scripted A02 conversation with a real successful example. Keep any fictional illustration identifiable as such. Use actual testimonials only after obtaining them and permission to use them.
5. **Adapt the selected composition to each placement.** Check legibility, cropping, and interface overlays in the final ad preview. A tall crop should be composed deliberately, not stretched from the feed version.

The gallery supplies **1080 × 1350 portrait feed**, **1080 × 1080 square**, and **1080 × 1920 Story** draft canvases. These are working design sizes, not a claim about Meta's latest mandatory or recommended export dimensions. Exact upload requirements, supported formats, and interface-safe areas need verification in the current Ads Guide and account. Final assets can be rendered at larger dimensions as appropriate.

The self-contained gallery exports draft PNGs of the artwork and downloads the selected image brief. Those exports are planning assets, not evidence that an ad has passed review. Changing “AI sommelier first” in the mixer changes supporting copy; it does not enable a product capability.

### Optional second round: short video and carousel

After choosing a message, create one simple product video before expanding formats widely. A possible 15-second sequence:

| Time | Scene | Copy or demonstration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 seconds | A bottle or an old label photo | “Loved the wine. Forgot the name?” |
| 3–8 seconds | Actual recording of saving a personal reaction | “Save what you tried—and what you thought.” |
| 8–12 seconds | Actual verified assistant interaction | “Bring your wine questions along.” |
| 12–15 seconds | Brand and one action | Launch updates, or download for iPhone, according to stage |

Use readable captions and communicate the benefit without requiring sound. This is consistent with Meta's published mobile-video creative guidance, though that source is older and is not evidence of current platform performance. [Meta mobile-video guidance, 2018](https://about.fb.com/fr/news/2018/08/les-publicites-videos-sur-mobile-facebook-propose-un-guide-des-bonnes-pratiques/)

A later carousel can explain three steps: save a wine, record your reaction, ask a question. Validate the actual flow before depicting it. The current gallery contains static feed and Story concepts; it does not pretend to include finished videos or an interactive carousel ad.

## 7. What to measure

Record the campaign stage, geography, message ID, visual ID, offer, dates, spend, and any material changes. A campaign URL could carry `utm_source=facebook`, `utm_medium=paid_social`, `utm_campaign=cn_va_prelaunch_r1`, and `utm_content=a01_memory_bottle_v1`. Use Instagram as the source for that placement where supported by the actual setup.

These URL labels help distinguish website visits. They do not automatically carry through an iOS install. Verify attribution and app events before treating reported conversions as connected to a specific ad. Do not add a tracking SDK as part of this documentation task.

| Funnel step | Useful measure | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention | Reach, impressions, frequency, outbound clicks | Diagnostic information about delivery and interest |
| Before-launch intent | Valid signup rate and cost per qualified signup | A real expression of interest; follow through to later activation |
| Installation | Attributed installs, where reliably available | An intermediate step rather than the product's lasting value |
| Journal value | First saved wine with a personal rating or reaction | Evidence the journal helped someone record an experience |
| Assistant value | Successful request plus usefulness feedback | A completed AI response alone does not establish helpfulness |
| Repeat value | Retrieval of a past wine, another note, or useful assistant activity within an appropriate follow-up window such as 30 days | Wine use is occasion-based; daily activity is not the main promise |
| Paid value | Trial starts, paid conversion, renewal, and support burden | Assess whether the paid assistance earns its cost |

Compare activation among new users from each source when attribution supports it. Combine small quantitative samples with direct feedback. If an ad attracts clicks but people misunderstand the app, revise the promise. If they understand it and then struggle to save a note, investigate the experience. Low activation by itself does not identify the cause.

For early economic decisions, calculate the cost per activated user and cost per paying user only when the underlying event counts are reliable. Evaluate these against observed net subscription contribution after fees, refunds, variable AI costs, and retention. Do not invent a lifetime-value number before there is evidence.

## 8. Research and platform limits

The prior positioning research found that personal AI help is already marketed by [Vivino](https://www.vivino.com/en/premium), and that [Sommo](https://sommo.app/features/wine-journal/) markets journals and tasting assistance. That supports testing the specific everyday benefit rather than assuming the presence of AI is a unique selling point. These are vendor descriptions, not independent quality comparisons.
