# Cork & Note: wine media, newsletters, and creator outreach

**Researched:** September 9, 2026. **Owner:** Nick. **Status:** research only; nobody contacted, no placements booked, no money spent.

## Start here

Start with **a wine-travel newsletter, a Virginia creator demonstration, and a few independent editorial pitches**. These give Cork & Note three ways to reach people who already visit wineries or keep tasting notes. Keep national magazines in the pipeline, but get audience and price information before committing the initial budget.

The strongest opening message is: **“Remember the wines you loved on your last winery visit.”** Show somebody recording a tasting, adding their own notes, and finding that wine later. Use scanning and cellar tracking as supporting demonstrations. An educator can make that experience understandable without having to endorse the AI sommelier as an authority.

## What to ask before paying

For a newsletter, request **delivered subscribers, geographic mix, unique sponsor clicks for the last three comparable sends, placement position, competing ads in the same email, send date, minimum booking, and total fee**. Ask whether click counts exclude automated/security clicks. Open rates alone are a weak buying signal; they do not demonstrate that people will try an app.

For a creator, request the current media kit and recent platform analytics: **audience countries/cities and age distribution, typical views on comparable posts, Story reach and link taps, and two relevant sponsored examples**. Ask for the median of recent comparable posts rather than just a viral example. Have the creator try the app before agreeing on the demonstration.

Put the deliverables in writing: account, format, posting date, number of Stories, link duration, live-post duration, revisions for factual accuracy, disclosure, reporting date, cancellation terms, and fees. Price **creator posting**, **content production**, and **permission to reuse the content in ads** separately. Narrow any app-category exclusivity by duration and scope. Use an iPhone-compatible reviewer build and never send an internal App Store review login to prospects.

Pay for disclosed advertising or honest sponsored content. Independent editorial consideration should come with **no required publication or favorable conclusion**. Free Pro access is still a material benefit when used in an endorsement; make that clear. Do not turn creator compensation into paid App Store ratings. FTC guidance supports clear disclosure of payments and gifts and endorsements based on real experience. [FTC influencer disclosure guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers), [FTC endorsements and reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews)

**TikTok needs a format-specific check.** TikTok states that branded-content and paid-ad policies differ; alcohol restrictions can affect wine-related creative. A wine journal's classification was not established by this research. Before contracting a TikTok post, confirm that the app and proposed demonstration are eligible under the relevant market and branded-content rules. Do not assume an organic wine video proves a paid endorsement is permitted. The listed creators can also be approached for Instagram or other eligible formats. [TikTok market-specific branded-content requirements](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/branded-content-policy-country-specific-requirements?lang=en-GB), [commercial-content disclosure](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/promoting-a-brand-product-or-service)

Before any paid placement, verify that its link leads to a working public App Store route or a clearly labeled beta signup. Reviewers can try TestFlight earlier, but paying to send consumers into an unfinished or confusing signup flow wastes the learning opportunity.

**Attribution is unfinished work.** PR #193 identifies a lack of attribution, and the current checkout did not reveal a campaign-tracking implementation. This report does not add one. Give each placement its own landing URL or redirect and campaign label before it runs; confirm that visits are recorded. Example labels: `wine_travelista_newsletter_01` and `sips_paige_instagram_01`.

Track these stages separately:

1. Publisher-reported delivery/reach and sponsor clicks.
2. Recorded landing visits and beta signups or store-link clicks.
3. App signups and **activation: first saved tasting within seven days of signup**.
4. A second meaningful session or another saved tasting within 30 days.
5. Paying customers and contribution after fees and variable costs, when billing is live.

Web campaign parameters do not automatically establish which iOS installs or in-app actions came from a placement. Until app-level attribution is implemented and tested, use a voluntary “How did you hear about us?” response or an instrumented invitation cohort, label the limitations, and report source-level website results separately from overall app activity. Do not label store-link clicks as installs.

Use cost per activated user for early comparisons, while recognizing that it is not paying-customer acquisition cost. For illustration only: a $300 placement yielding 100 landing visits and 20 attributable first tastings costs $15 per activation. If it later yields two paying customers, acquisition cost is $150 per paying customer. That is not evidence of profitable acquisition at the planned subscription price; renewal and margin data would be needed. Set an acceptable acquisition ceiling from actual customer economics before scaling.
